Stars

Discovering how stars are born, change, and eventually die.

What Are Stars?

Stars are huge balls of hot gas that produce their own light and heat. They are found throughout the universe and come in many different sizes, colors, and temperatures. Stars are important because they provide energy and help create many of the elements found in space.

Our Sun: The Star Closest to Earth

The Sun is the closest star to Earth and is the center of our solar system. It provides the light and heat that make life on Earth possible. The Sun is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Deep inside the Sun, a process called nuclear fusion takes place. This process turns hydrogen into helium and releases huge amounts of energy. That energy travels through space and reaches Earth as sunlight and heat.

The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old and is considered a middle aged star. It is expected to continue shining for about another 5 billion years before it begins to change into a red giant.

Different Types of Stars

Not all stars are the same. Some stars are much larger and hotter than our Sun, while others are smaller and cooler. The color of a star can give scientists clues about its temperature. Blue stars are usually the hottest, while red stars are cooler.

Some stars are called red dwarfs because they are small and cooler than the Sun. Other stars, called giant stars and supergiants, are much larger and brighter. Each type of star has different characteristics and goes through its own life cycle.

Scientists study stars to learn more about how the universe formed and how it continues to change.

Interesting Facts About Stars

  • The Sun is the closest star to Earth.
  • Stars are made mostly of hydrogen and helium.
  • Blue stars are hotter than red stars.
  • Some stars are millions of times brighter than the Sun.
  • Many stars are grouped together in galaxies.

The Life Cycle of a Star

1. Nebula

A star begins in a large cloud of gas and dust called a nebula. Gravity pulls the material together.

2. Protostar

As the gas and dust come together, the center becomes hotter and denser. A young star called a protostar begins to form.

3. Main Sequence Star

The star begins producing energy through nuclear fusion. It spends most of its life in this stage, just like our Sun.

4. Red Giant

When a star runs low on hydrogen fuel, it expands and becomes much larger. Its outer layers begin to cool.

5. Final Stages

Smaller stars lose their outer layers and become white dwarfs. Much larger stars can explode as supernovas and leave behind neutron stars or black holes.
